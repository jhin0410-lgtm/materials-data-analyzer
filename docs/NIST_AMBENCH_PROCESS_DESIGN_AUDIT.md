# NIST AM-Bench 2018-02 Process-Design Identifiability Audit

## Purpose

The process–characterization handoff now proves that ten NIST AMMT trace identities, process settings, and optical-metrology outcomes can be joined with complete provenance. That does not by itself make the experiment suitable for regression, causal interpretation, or optimization.

This audit evaluates the process design before any model is fitted.

## Run

The audit accepts the `integrated_sample_table.csv` generated by either the local NIST case workflow or the cross-repository consumer workflow.

```powershell
python scripts/audit_nist_ambench_2018_02_process_design.py `
  --integrated-table outputs/cross-repository-nist-ambench-2018-02/integrated_sample_table.csv `
  --output outputs/nist-ambench-process-design-audit
```

The output directory must be new or empty.

## Checks

The audit records:

- sample count and unique process-condition count;
- a one-to-one identity contract between each `case_id` and one physical `(actual_laser_power_w, scan_speed_mm_s)` condition;
- trace replication per condition and pure-error degrees of freedom;
- observed laser-power and scan-speed levels;
- observed versus possible two-factor combinations;
- missing power–speed combinations;
- availability of matched-speed power contrasts and within-power speed contrasts;
- sample-level and condition-level power–speed correlation;
- standardized design-matrix rank for:
  - main effects;
  - main effects plus interaction;
  - quadratic response surface;
- condition-level residual degrees of freedom;
- observed process bounds and extrapolation restrictions;
- a machine-readable readiness decision.

The audit rejects both ambiguous directions of identity: one `case_id` mapped to multiple process conditions, or multiple `case_id` values mapped to the same physical power–speed condition. Without this guard, condition count, pure-error degrees of freedom, and factorial coverage could be overstated.

No response value is fitted or recomputed.

## Expected NIST result

```text
samples:                         10
unique process conditions:        3
replicate counts:              3/3/4
pure-error degrees of freedom:    7
power levels:                     2
speed levels:                     3
possible 2 x 3 combinations:      6
observed combinations:            3
factorial coverage:              50%
```

The main-effects matrix has three parameters and rank three, but it is saturated across the three unique conditions. Its condition-level residual degrees of freedom are zero. Therefore the coefficients are algebraically estimable but model adequacy, curvature, and a scientifically validated independent-effect interpretation are unavailable.

The interaction model has four parameters but rank three. The quadratic response-surface model has six parameters but rank three. Both are non-identifiable.

## Why power and speed are not independently validated

There is no scan speed measured at both power levels. The low-power condition exists only at 400 mm/s, while the high-power conditions exist at 800 and 1200 mm/s.

A speed contrast exists between 800 and 1200 mm/s at the high-power level, but there is no matched-speed contrast that isolates power. The design is also only half of the possible 2×3 factor combinations.

Trace replication estimates within-condition variability. It does not create missing process conditions or resolve factor confounding.

## Scientific closeout

**Evidence level: Diagnostic**

Supported:

- descriptive comparison of the three observed conditions;
- within-condition repeatability review;
- explicit identification of missing design support;
- determination that interaction and curvature are not identifiable.

Not supported:

- independent causal effects of laser power and scan speed;
- interaction or response-surface claims;
- predictive generalization;
- process optimization;
- extrapolation outside the observed process bounds;
- engineering-release decisions.

Evidence that would change the conclusion requires additional independently traceable conditions that cross power and speed at shared levels, preserve replication, add center or curvature support, and include held-out validation.
